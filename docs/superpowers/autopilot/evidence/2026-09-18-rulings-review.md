## Reviewer A: items 22, 23, 1

**F1 — Item 1. The age private key is not on this host, and `.meta.json` carries no ciphertext digest. Precondition (a) as written cannot be executed, and option 1 may be irreversible in fact.**
Draft claim: "after (a) I confirm the NAS pulled both w37 `.dump.age` files (sha256 against `.meta.json`)".
`/srv/sports-harness/backups/partitions/harness-partitions-orderbook_events_y2026w37-20260914T192207Z.meta.json` records `"path": ".../...dump"`, `"sha256": "dcce2a41..."`, `"bytes": 4398604194` — that is the **plaintext** dump (the `.age` is 4399678266 bytes). There is no digest of the ciphertext anywhere, so "sha256 the pulled `.age` against `.meta.json`" is not a check that exists. The only way to validate a pulled ciphertext is to decrypt it and hash the plaintext. `secrets/backup_age_key` is absent from `/srv/sports-harness/secrets/` (only `anthropic_api_key`, `dashboard_token`, `kalshi_key_id`, `kalshi_private_key.pem`, `odds_api_key`) and from `/home/trey/dev/sports/secrets/` (empty); only the recipient `deploy/backup_age.pub` is present. Roadmap Secrets says the key was "generated by the loop on the Mac" — a retired host.
Corrected wording: *"(a) I produce the age private key and prove it decrypts `harness-partitions-orderbook_events_y2026w37-*.dump.age` to a plaintext whose sha256 equals the `sha256` in its `.meta.json`. Until that succeeds, no partition is dropped and no plaintext `.dump` is deleted."*

**F2 — Item 1. `drill.sh` does not decrypt anything; running it on the w37 dump proves the least interesting half.**
Draft: "the loop runs `drill.sh` on the w37 orderbook dump and journals the `drill ok` row … a partition dump has never been restored anywhere; the `.ok` marker proves structure, not restorability."
`deploy/backup/drill.sh:19-27` takes a plaintext `.dump`; its header (`drill.sh:9-16`) states outright: "Proof that the *ciphertext* decrypts is the Mac half and lives elsewhere: the private key is not on the NAS." So the proposed drill exercises the 4.4 GB plaintext that sits on the same filesystem as the data being dropped, not the `.age` that is the only surviving copy. Separately, the proposal itself (`reports/2026-09-15-storage-retention-proposal.md`, "What the spec and the milestones need kept" and Recommendation (b)) specifies an **off-host decrypt** drill; the draft silently weakened it to a local plaintext restore.
Corrected wording: keep drill.sh in the plan, but as step two of a decrypt-then-restore: `age -d` the ciphertext to a scratch plaintext, sha256 it against `.meta.json`, then `drill.sh` that file.

**F3 — Item 1. A "drill ok" row can be recorded for a drill that never ran, and a partition drill verifies structure only.**
`drill.sh:29` defaults `MIN_FREE_PCT=30`; `drill.sh:91-95` prints `SKIP drill free=…` and **`exit 0`**. Anything journaling on exit status alone records success for a skipped drill. And for `counts_snapshot: "none"` (which this unit is — `"counts":{}` in the sidecar) `drill.sh:142-154` prints `COMPARED 0 MISMATCHES 0 / ROWS_MATCH n/a` and exits: the drill proves `pg_restore` completes, not that the restored partition holds its 139.8 M rows.
Corrected wording: *"the drill row is written only on a `RESTORE_OK` line, and the ruling records that a partition drill returns `ROWS_MATCH n/a` by design — restorability is proven, row count is not."* Disk is not the obstacle: `/`, `/srv` and `/var/lib/docker` are one 952 G filesystem with 773 G free, so an 84 GB restore fits.

**F4 — Item 22. `CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY` on `orders` is far riskier than "takes a minute", because of this loop's own long transactions.**
Draft: "an index build on a 1 GB table concurrently takes a minute."
CIC waits out every transaction that can see `orders`. The executor runs one outer transaction per step (`loop.py` `step()`, `begin_nested()` savepoints throughout), and item 23's own evidence is steps of **184/253/204/116 s**. `migrations/env.py:108-114` sets `statement_timeout = 300s` and `lock_timeout = 5s` on the migration connection. A CIC issued during or near a game window can plausibly exceed 300 s across its two waits and leave `indisvalid = false` — the exact failure `harness/db/migrate.py:130-133` documents ("The half-built index stayed in the catalogue with `indisvalid = false`, where the next release's `if not exists` would skip it"). And `orders` is **not** in `BULK_TABLES` (`migrations/env.py:28-29`), so the deny-list at `harness/db/migrate.py:165-172` does *not* protect it: the next full deploy's `migrate ensure` would silently `REINDEX INDEX` a 952 MB table under ACCESS EXCLUSIVE with a 5 s lock timeout.
Corrected wording: *"…build it in a quiet hour with the executor's loops short, with `statement_timeout` raised for that statement only, and verify `pg_index.indisvalid` immediately after. If it comes back invalid, that is a stop for me, not a retry — the cleanup is a DROP (gate 3) and `migrate ensure` would otherwise reindex it non-concurrently on the next full deploy."*

**F5 — Item 22. The "145 ms" is unsourced arithmetic, and the EXPLAIN number is larger than the total it is supposed to explain.**
Draft: "EXPLAIN ANALYZE of the count … 153.9 ms (execution 164 ms) … That is the 145 ms."
154 ms > 145 ms. Either the 145 ms is an average with a warmer cache, or the denominator is wrong. The measured quantity is `exec.phase_place_ms` (`loop.py:182`: "`phase_place_ms` `_apply` alone (row 82's placement timer)"), and `_apply` covers `Cancel`, `Expire`, `CapGate` and `Skip` too (`loop.py:2152-2180`), none of which call `orders_for_intent`. The ruling should cite `phase_place_ms / placed` explicitly.
I did check the competing hypotheses and they are **not** it, which strengthens the draft: `orders_for_intent` (`store.py:1011-1013`) is a plain `text()` and `Session.execute` **does not autoflush** a plain text statement (`store.py:1348-1352` says so in as many words); `_WORKING_ORDERS` (`store.py:348+`) is a raw projection, so there is no large ORM identity map to flush; `insert_order`'s `on_conflict_do_nothing … RETURNING` (`store.py:1061-1074`) and `uq_open_order` maintenance (`harness/db/schema.py:229`) are single-index-lookup costs. The seq scan is the plausible dominant term — but say "the count query, measured at 154 ms cold against a 952 MB heap," not "that is the 145 ms."

**F6 — Item 22. An app-only alternative with identical stored values was not considered, and the by-hand statement creates catalogue drift.**
Draft: "it is NOT batched placement writes: it is one additive concurrent index … Batched placement writes are not needed."
Batched *writes* is the wrong strawman. The unconsidered option is a batched **read**: one `select intent_id, count(*) from orders where intent_id = any(:ids) group by 1` per loop instead of 150 sequential counts — one 154 ms scan a loop, not 22 s, with byte-identical `client_order_id` values so long as the in-loop increment is applied when an intent places twice in one step. That is app-only (`deploy.md` step 3 rejects app-only for migrations, not for this), deployable without the Monday window, and carries no invalid-index risk. Against it: it is executor value-path code in the module fix 79 just changed — the same objection the draft uses to refuse code for item 23. That tension should be resolved explicitly rather than left implicit.
On the by-hand statement: it is additive, so not gate 3 (roadmap "Invariants" 5; SKILL.md gate 3 only names *non-concurrent* index on a bulk table). But `ix_intents_created` and `ix_quotes_run_market` show the required shape — the index is declared in three places at once (`harness/db/schema.py:393`, `harness/db/models.py:473`, `migrations/versions/0012_phase6d_sustained_eval.py:130`) "or `tests/test_alembic.py`'s catalogue diff fails" (`schema.py:386-392`). Precedent: `0002_phase45.py:125` (`ix_orders_key_placed`, CONCURRENTLY, on `orders` itself) and fix 45's `0007_raw_events_lookup.py`. So a hand-built index with no code behind it is an **undeclared** index that any rebuilt database silently lacks.
Corrected wording: *"…and if I approve the by-hand statement, the model/`schema.py`/migration declaration lands in the same release anyway; the hand statement only moves the build earlier, it never replaces the declaration."*

**F7 — Item 23. Memoizing per `(ticker, cursor)` does not fix Sunday, and part (ii) is not value-identical.**
Draft: "memoizing `_sim_book`'s historical book per (ticker, cursor) within a loop (value-identical; 5,532 rows -> 1,338 loads on Sunday's cohort)".
The memo is correct in principle — `_sim_book`'s historical branch (`loop.py:2074-2086`) derives everything from `row.ticker` and `state.cursor_event_id`: `at = store.event_ts(cursor)`, then `load_book_at(ticker, at, newest_ws_connect(at))`. Both extra verdicts inside `load_book_at` (`book.py:653-671`) — `_gapped_at` and `_mark_session_boundary` — are functions of `(ticker, instant)` only, so two rows sharing a cursor genuinely get the same book. The `.copy()` requirement the draft flags is real (`book.py:378-384`; the historical branch returns the freshly-built book uncopied, unlike the `base.copy()`/`anchor.copy()` branches). Two corrections:
- **Arithmetic.** 1,338 loads × the draft's own 127-225 ms is still 3-5 minutes. The memo turns a 12-20 min loop into a 3-5 min loop. It is not a fix; say so.
- **(ii) is not a shared reconstruction, it is a different verdict.** The machinery already exists — `advance_book_at` (`book.py:674-700`) — and it is *not* equivalent to `load_book_at`: it adds the `_dropped_delta_at` probe (`out.mark_dirty("event_age")`) and a re-anchor branch that `book_at`/`load_book_at` deliberately do not carry (`book.py:640-646`). Advancing one reconstruction across a ticker's cursors therefore changes `dirty`/`dirty_cause` on some books and, through them, stored fills. Frame (ii) as "a value-changing proposal that needs a replay comparison", not as a free win.
- Also correct "value-identical" to "value-identical within a loop's transaction": the memo pins the first read, and a late-arriving tape row with `ts <= at` would otherwise change the second.

**F8 — Item 23. Raising `DELTA_BATCH_LIMIT` is neither a setting nor low-risk — it is a value-path change that makes things worse.**
`store.py:846`, `DELTA_BATCH_LIMIT = 20_000`, a module constant; the per-ticker size is in-memory and adaptive (fix 26, `loop.py:596-600`, `1762`, `1800-1821`). It is value-path: a truncated read sets `lagging` (`loop.py:1815`), and `lagging` gates the close — `loop.py:2497`, `done = (row.ticker not in lagging and ((row.expiry is not None and row.expiry <= now) or filled >= row.contracts))`. Change the limit and you change which rows get `nw_done` on which loop. Worse, fix 26's own note (`store.py:848-855`) says 20,000 rows "needs thousands of pages and cannot" finish inside the 10 s `EXEC_STATEMENT_TIMEOUT_MS`; raising it converts `lagging` tickers into **`unread`** ones, and an unread ticker's rows are skipped entirely (`loop.py:1085-1093`), which is strictly worse than being walked. There is no operational (non-code) lever here: the expiring cohort is exempt from `exec_nw_budget_ms` by construction (`loop.py:1101-1102`, `1271-1276`), so no setting bounds the loop.

**F9 — Item 23. The evidence chain is right in mechanism but overstated in scope, and one blocker is missing.**
Draft: "per_row cause `book_query` … every expiring row is on the per-row path."
The mechanism checks out: expiring rows are walked ahead of the rotation (`loop.py:1266-1281`), `_writes_nothing` refuses the historical read and returns `PER_ROW_BOOK_QUERY` (`loop.py:1494-1498`), and the per-row stage then pays `event_ts` + `load_book_at` — potentially **twice** per row, since `_sim_book` is called for both tracks (`loop.py:2016`, `2050`). But: book_query 1,155 exceeds expiring 1,104 in the draft's own first loop, so the cause counter is not an expiring-row counter; expiring rows whose cursor equals `base.last_event_id` batch normally. And `exec.tape_lag_tickers` counts **only** truncated reads (`loop.py:1815`, `1865`); tickers whose read timed out (`unread`) or are in §0.14 backoff (`deferred`) block their rows more completely (`loop.py:1085-1093`) and are reported separately (`heartbeat["tape_deferred"]`, `stats.errors`). The draft's four-loop reading never quotes those, so "why the cohort stayed open" is not established — 6 lagging tickers out of 20+ does not explain 868 rows persisting.
Corrected wording for the design read: add "(0) confirm from `exec` errors / `tape_deferred` / the per-ticker split which of lagging, unread and deferred actually held each un-closed row, before proposing anything."

**F10 — Item 1. Dropping w37 creates a live insert failure mode this box has already experienced.**
After `DROP TABLE orderbook_events_y2026w37`, any insert with `ts` in Sep 7-13 raises `no partition of relation "orderbook_events" found for row`. That is not hypothetical here: roadmap "2026-09-14 (journal 175, host outage)" records the RTC reading 2020-01-01 at boot and runs 14485/14486 stamped Sun 15:31 but written Mon 02:21. A clock regression after a reboot would turn a silent mis-stamp into a hard recorder failure. Worth one line in the ruling (the `clock = unsynced` guard and `systemd-time-wait-sync` are the mitigation already in place).

**F11 — Item 1. `DETACH PARTITION` takes ACCESS EXCLUSIVE on the live parent; the ruling needs a lock timeout.**
Partitions are ordinary declarative range children (`harness/db/partition.py:191-192`, `alter table {table} attach partition {legacy} for values from … to …`; `harness/db/schema.py:75-81`), so `DETACH` + `DROP TABLE` is the right pair. But a plain `DETACH` queues for ACCESS EXCLUSIVE on `orderbook_events` and, while queued, blocks every subsequent reader and the WS sink's inserts behind it. Add: *"`set lock_timeout = '5s'` first so it fails fast instead of queueing; do `venue_trades_y2026w37` (1.9 GB) first as the rehearsal; consider `DETACH PARTITION CONCURRENTLY`, noting it cannot run in a transaction block and leaves a DETACH-PENDING state to FINALIZE if interrupted."*
The "reads by week" check comes out clean, as the proposal claimed: every tape read on main is `ts`-bounded (`harness/report/tables.py:1064-1070`, `1206-1210`, `1341-1343`; `harness/dashboard/app.py:388-420`), and `harness/ops/checks.py:28` forbids checks from touching `orderbook_events` at all. The residual risk is silence, not error — a 6C/6D report re-run over a Sep 7-13 window would return empty rather than fail. `harness/rescore.py:148` and `harness/capsule.py:198` are the two consumers that would need a `pg_restore`; the order-157 capsule is committed to the repo, so it survives.

**F12 — Item 1. Unit mismatch in the numbers the decision rests on.**
Draft: "`orderbook_events_y2026w37` 79 GB … database 148 GB". The proposal (same table, sealed, cannot shrink) says 84.3 GB and "139.6 GB (130 GiB)". 84.3 GB = 78.5 GiB. The draft is quoting GiB against the proposal's GB. Pick one and label it, or the "frees 86 GB" line and the 480/600 GB thresholds are not comparable.

**F13 — Items 22/23 interaction, unstated.** The placement count runs inside the same long step transaction that CIC must wait behind, and 150 orders × ~145 ms ≈ 22 s is 9-12 % of those 184-253 s kickoff loops. Fixing item 22 shortens item 23's loops slightly and shortens the transactions CIC has to wait out — so the index is best built *after* the placement cost is gone, or in a genuinely quiet hour.

**Verdicts.** Item 22: AMEND. Item 23: AMEND. Item 1: REPLACE the preconditions. The one thing the user must know: the age private key for `deploy/backup_age.pub` is not on this host and `.meta.json` hashes only the plaintext — so today nobody can prove the w37 `.dump.age` is readable, and dropping the partition on the strength of an `.ok` marker and a plaintext drill would be an unrecoverable delete of 139.8 M tape rows.

## Reviewer B: items 21, 19

**1. [Critical] The draft's eligibility criterion, read against the code, is not the criterion it intends — it deletes 95 % of the scheduled set, including ~591 k *completed* evaluations per variant.**
Draft claim: "the scheduled ELIGIBLE set is markets with a sharp line **at enumeration** (a gap row with a fair input)."
At enumeration the only gap rows that exist are the **direct** ones, and `gaps.py:194-196` (`if phase == "direct" and fair is None: continue`) writes a direct row *only* where a fair already exists. `pipeline.py:471-478` builds the cells from `direct_rows` + `market_order`; every market without a direct row gets `Cell()` (all-null). So "eligible at enumeration" = `direct_rows` = the "real cells" only.
Arithmetic on the draft's own numbers (7 variants): all-null scheduled 6,295,968/7 = **899,424 per variant** of 943,190 total (**95.4 %**); real cells 43,766, completed 43,585 (0.996 — matches the draft's 0.988-1.000). Of the all-null units, 4,136,587/7 = **590,941 per variant complete** — they got a *derived* fair in stage 5 and are classified `completed` by `pipeline.py:569-575`. The draft's wording throws all of those away: denominator 943,190 → 43,766.
The set the draft actually means is "the unit's gap row ended the run carrying a fair" (`market_gap_snapshots.no_fair_reason is null`) — a **completion-time** predicate, not an enumeration-time one. Cross-check: 637,374 (null reason) + 305,816 (`no_sharp_line`) = **943,190** = the per-variant scheduled total exactly, i.e. scheduled per variant ≡ one unit per gap row, and the contract today is measuring "share of enumerated markets that ended with a fair."
*Corrected wording, if this route is taken at all:* "…the reported denominator excludes units whose gap row closed with `no_fair_reason = 'no_sharp_line'`; those units stay scheduled and are reported as a named excluded class with their own share per cell."

**2. [Critical] Internal contradiction, and the amended row would pass by construction.**
The ruling text says both "the scheduled ELIGIBLE set is markets with a sharp line" and "the no-sharp-line class **stays scheduled**-and-reported". Those are different instruments. Worse: the draft offers "EVERY real cell reads 0.988-1.000" as reassurance, but that is *definitional* — a direct gap row exists only where a fair exists — so per-cell coverage carries no information today, and after the amendment the headline ratio would read ~0.99 by construction, able to detect only budget skips (`budget_stage_skipped`/`variant_skipped`) and dead ticks. The draft's stated objection to (b) ("the row stops measuring anything") applies with more force to its own (a).

**3. [High] The fixable defect here is the instrument's *grain*, and it is code, not a check.**
Decision 4 asks for coverage "by sport, time to kickoff, feed, market and variant". With 95 % of rows in the all-null cell that grain is non-functional, which is why the draft has to say "the characterization needs `market_gap_snapshots` joined to `venue_markets`." It does not: `harness/pricing/gaps.py:126-131` already holds `market` and `game` for every row in `market_order` when it captures the ordering, so `sport`, `ttk_bucket` and `market_type` are knowable at enumeration for *every* unit at zero query cost (only `feed` genuinely needs a gap row). Populating them is additive, keeps `evaluation_cells`' "fixed at enumeration, reused at completion" rule (`coverage.py:18-22`), leaves the ratio identical (cells sum to the same totals), stays inside ruling I6's 336-cell bound, and is **not gate 13** — it changes no check. It answers "which markets" permanently and on the next game day.
*Recommended replacement for the ruling's first move:* do this first; decide the definition afterwards on the breakdown.

**4. [High] The ruling pre-commits to an amendment whose predicate the draft admits is unknown — and `no_sharp_line` does not mean what the draft says.**
`harness/pricing/fair.py:311-341`: the label is written whenever `_build_model` returns None for the game (then *every* remaining shape counts) or `mu_total is None` for a total; the model is built from `latest_book_lines(..., lookback_s)` over `SHARP_BOOKS = ("pinnacle","betonlineag","lowvig")` (`harness/pricing/direct.py:8`). A staleness or recorder regression lands in exactly the same bucket the draft proposes to exclude — the "band on the share is the guard" claim is unproven until the class is decomposed.

**5. [Medium] "About one third of matched markets have no sharp line" is units of work, not markets.**
